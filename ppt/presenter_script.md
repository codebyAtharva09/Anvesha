## Slide 1 — Title (≈ 30 s)

**Say:**
"We are Regnum Carya. Our problem statement is SIH26169 from ISRO: a virtual camera tracking
system for coarse alignment of mobile free-space optical terminals. Our solution is called
ANVESHA — Sanskrit for *search* — because we found that the hard part of this problem is not
detecting a bright spot, it is deciding where to look, how wide to look, and what not to chase."

**Likely questions**
- *Why the name?* — Anveṣaṇa means search / quest; the core contribution is the search layer.
- *Is this software only?* — Yes, PS category Software. The tracker exchanges only frames, encoder / IMU
  readings and rate commands with the simulator, so a real camera and pan-tilt can replace it later (slide 5).

## Slide 2 — Idea / Proposed solution (≈ 90 s)

**Say:**
1. *The physics (left panel):* "The screen is 12.5° × 12.5°. The default 4° × 3° camera sees
   7.7 % of it. At 5 °/s the gimbal can slew at most 10° in two seconds, but a blind raster at
   the base FOV needs roughly 40–50° of slew — about 8–10 s worst case. So ≤ 2 s from a random
   start is not a tuning problem; it is a *where-to-look* problem."
2. *The loop (middle):* "Camera → detect → estimate → belief and planner → control → gimbal.
   The orange block is what is new: one probability map of where the beacon can be. Every frame
   in which we do not see the beacon removes probability from the area we looked at. The planner
   picks the next pointing and FOV (1×, 2× or 3× the base FOV) that gives the highest detection
   probability per second, using the noise we actually measure in the image."
3. *Innovation (right):* read the three titles only — belief-driven search, disturbance-aware FOV,
   one belief across the whole search–track–loss–re-acquire lifecycle.
4. *Table:* "Typical public approaches use a fixed spiral, a rule-switched wide FOV, or restart
   the spiral after a loss. We replace each with a probabilistic decision."
5. Point to the measured badge: "Measured on identical seeds, 77 % of our 48 runs acquire within
   2 s versus 12–15 % for three spiral baselines. Slide 4 shows where we still fail."

**Likely questions**
- *Isn't a wider FOV just cheating?* — The PS makes FOV user-defined (default 4° × 3°). A wider FOV
  costs resolution: at 3× a 10 px beacon covers ~3 camera pixels, so it can vanish in noise. That is
  exactly why the FOV is *chosen* from predicted detection probability instead of fixed. The ablation
  study (adaptive FOV off; belief replaced by spiral) quantifies how much of the gain comes from FOV
  vs the belief — it appears on slide 4 only once `results/ablation/summary.json` exists; if it is
  not on the slide, say so plainly rather than guessing the split.
- *What is "negative information"?* — Not seeing the beacon is evidence: Bayes' rule multiplies the
  probability of every covered cell by (1 − Pd). Classical search theory (Koopman, Stone).
- *Where did 40–50° come from?* — 13–17 tiles of 4° × 3° to cover 12.5° × 12.5°, with tile-to-tile
  slews; the exact figure depends on the pattern, which is why we quote a range and the order of
  magnitude (8–10 s) rather than a single number.
- *Is this novel?* — We say only: based on our review of 134 papers and 20 public SIH26169
  repositories we did not identify a coarse-PAT system that combines these three elements. IMM,
  MF-CFAR, CNN verification and feed-forward control are prior art and cited as such.

## Slide 3 — Technical approach (≈ 75 s)

**Say:**
"Top band: a 240 Hz physics simulation — beacon on seven trajectories, platform motion with an IMU,
and every PS disturbance: Gaussian, Poisson and salt-and-pepper noise, jitter, haze, fog, rain, low
light and turbulence. The virtual focal-plane-array camera is 640 × 480 at 30 Hz; the gimbal is rate
limited to 5 °/s with lag and latency.
The navy row is the tracking chain: perception with a matched filter and CFAR threshold plus a
small CNN verifier; gated association; an IMM estimator whose measurement noise adapts to the
jitter it measures; a supervisor state machine; and a 60 Hz predictive controller with IMU
feed-forward.
Orange is the belief search, connected to the supervisor in both directions. Amber is Benchmark-2:
an evaluator's .mp4 bypasses the camera and enters the same perception and estimator. Green is
the metrics layer — it is the only place that sees ground truth — and it writes the per-frame CSV,
JSON summary and HTML report automatically. The screenshot is the live mission-control GUI."

**Likely questions**
- *Why Python and not C++?* — Profiled: processing stays far above 20 FPS on a CPU (slide 4); ROI
  processing and vectorised OpenCV keep it cheap. Python keeps the code readable for ISRO reviewers.
- *Where is the AI?* — (1) a 6.8 k-parameter CNN that verifies candidate patches (ONNX, CPU),
  trained on synthetic patches; held-out recall 90 %, false-positive rate 23 % — in-distribution
  only, so it is a verifier, not the primary detector; (2) the probabilistic belief/planner and IMM
  are model-based estimation, not deep learning — we do not claim otherwise.
- *Why not YOLO / Siamese trackers?* — A beacon spot has no texture; those models add latency and
  domain-shift risk without a measured benefit for a 5–20 px square.
- *Why IMM?* — Seven trajectories mix constant velocity, turning and abrupt manoeuvres; one
  constant-velocity Kalman filter lags on figure-8 and random motion. IMM is prior art.
- *Does ground truth leak into the tracker?* — No. Truth flows only into the metrics module.
- *Is the .exe ready?* — Not yet; PyInstaller packaging is planned (labelled "planned" on slide 4).

## Slide 4 — Feasibility and viability (≈ 90 s)

**Say:**
"This slide is our evidence, including where we fail. Left: ANVESHA on 16 scenarios, three seeds
each, against every PS limit — green meets it, red does not. It meets all PS checks in 9 of 16
scenarios; the three baselines, run on the same seeds, meet all checks in none.
Middle: 77 % of 48 runs acquire within 2 s versus 12–15 % for spiral baselines. [If shown: the
ablation line says what each part buys.] Re-acquisition after two scripted occlusions averages
0.07 s with nothing unrecovered. In Benchmark-2 video mode on a 2000 × 2000, 30 fps clip the
raw centroid RMSE is 0.14 px at 29 FPS end-to-end including video decoding.
Bottom: our measured gaps. In dense fog and rain we did not acquire at all; look-alike distractors
and the all-maxima case cause false locks; LOS error exceeds 10 px under vibration, fast
manoeuvres and occlusion. And one limit nobody can meet with a coarse gimbal: ±20 px white jitter
alone is 16.3 px RMS, above the 10 px limit. We report LOS error and image error separately so
the evaluator can see which part is correctable."

**Likely questions**
- *Why do you fail in fog when you claim disturbance-aware FOV?* — The FOV logic decides *how wide*
  to look; in the G_fog scenario the beacon's contrast falls below our detection threshold even at
  the base FOV, so no FOV choice helps. The fix is in detection (veil removal / threshold), which is
  our next work item. We chose to show this rather than tune the scenario away.
- *Baselines look weak — are they strawmen?* — They represent typical public designs (threshold /
  blob / CNN detector + Kalman + PID + spiral). PID gains were grid-searched over kp, ki, kd on
  circular, figure-8 and random runs; best gains used for all baselines. The acquisition gap is
  mostly physics: spiral at 4° × 3° cannot cover the screen in 2 s.
- *Only 3 seeds?* — Yes; enough to show large effects (77 % vs 12–15 %), not to claim small ones.
  Scenario-level values are means of 3 seeds; the matrix shows k/3 successes explicitly.
- *What does "Loop FPS" include?* — The whole simulation step including scene rendering, on a
  2-core CPU with two benchmark runs in parallel; the tracking pipeline alone is faster. Lowest
  loop FPS was 34 (Poisson scenario) — above 20.
- *16.3 px — measured or computed?* — Computed: uniform ±20 px has σ = 20/√3 = 11.5 px per axis,
  radial RMS = √2 × 11.5 = 16.3 px. The E_jitter row (±10 px) shows the same split measured:
  image error 8.9 px vs LOS 4.6 px.
- *Why is the video centroid error so small?* — It is the raw per-frame measurement on our own
  generated video with known truth; evaluator videos may differ. The filtered estimate error is
  larger (≈5 px) because the IMM smooths; we log both.

## Slide 5 — Impact and benefits (≈ 60 s)

**Say:**
"Three audiences. ISRO and DoS PAT teams can design and regression-test coarse-PAT logic for mobile
ground terminals, UAV or HAP relays and LEO downlinks before hardware exists. Students and
researchers get an open, seeded testbed without an optics lab. Indian FSOC start-ups can move the
same algorithms onto a real camera and pan-tilt, because the tracker only exchanges frames, encoder and IMU
readings and rate commands with the world.
The benefit the PS itself names is cost: expensive cameras, pan-tilt units and optical benches are
needed only for final validation. Acquisition time matters in practice — an on-orbit optical link
demonstration reported a 22-second acquisition. Our roadmap: today software-in-the-loop; next real
footage through the video mode; then a hardware-in-the-loop rig; finally hand-off to a fine-steering
stage that removes the residual jitter."

**Likely questions**
- *How much money does it save?* — We have not costed a specific rig and will not quote a figure;
  the claim is qualitative: algorithm iteration no longer needs the hardware.
- *What does HIL need?* — A camera driver that delivers frames, a pan-tilt driver that accepts rate
  commands and returns encoder angles, an IMU, and a beacon (LED or laser); these replace the simulator
  behind the same boundary the engine uses today. Perception, estimation, search and control stay unchanged;
  truth-based metrics would need an external reference.
- *Environmental benefit is weak?* — It is minor; listed last and stated modestly.
